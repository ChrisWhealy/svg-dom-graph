Complex numbers

A data node can hold one or more complex numbers, either as single or double precision floating point values. Each number can be written in whichever format suits the task at hand. Integer types are not supported for use in complex numbers.

The Argand diagrams below draw the complex plane: real are plotted along the horizontal axis and imaginary values along the vertical axis. Every complex is then displayed as a point on the plain. Each of the different ways of formatting a complex number provide you with a different way to describe that point's position on the complex plane.

Rectangular

Rectangular coordinates identify a point on the complex plane by tracing the sides of a rectangle anchored to the origin.

So for the complex number 3 - 4i, you travel 3 units in the positive direction along the horizontal axis, then 4 units in the negative direction along the vertical axis.

When displayed in a DataNode, a small, scale drawing of this rectangle appears to the left of the numeric value.

Polar

Polar coordinates describe the position of a point on the complex plane by telling you first how far that point lies away from the origin, and second, in what direction you should travel. The convention is that rotation is always the number of degrees anticlockwise from the positive X axis. So a of rotation 90° means travelling due north up the positive Y axis.

In polar coordinates, the same complex number used above (3 - 4i) would be represented as 5 units away from the origin, at an of angle 306.87°. The 5 is calculated by knowing that we have a right-angled triangle whose orthogonal sides are of length 3 and 4. So according to the Pythagorean formula, this give a hypotenuse length of 5.

When displayed in a DataNode, a small, scale drawing of an arrow pointing the correct direction appears to the left of the numeric value.

Trigonometric and exponential

r (cos θ + i sin θ) is much like the polar form, but with the position of the point calculated as the cosine and sine of the angle of rotation (both scaled by the factor r)

Euler's formula describes the same thing but calculates the angle of rotation using the formula eiθ, and the length as r.

The value of θ is always in radians.

Log-polar

The pair (ln r, θ). It is the real and imaginary parts of the complex logarithm ln z = ln r + iθ, so a product, which multiplies the moduli, adds the first number of each pair as well as the second. The log of the modulus of zero is -inf.

Matrix

The matrix representation describes how complex number a + bi turns and stretches a unit length on the plane. It is shown as a 2×2 matrix with a on one diagonal and -b/b on the other diagonal.

Hover over a node to see its type: complex64 has two f64 parts, and complex32 has two f32 parts. Under the hexadecimal and binary formats, a complex number shows the bits first of its real part, then of its imaginary part.

One number, seven ways

Drag the slider under the diagram to change the real part a, and the one beside it to change the imaginary part b. Each is exactly as long as the axis it lies along, so its thumb is directly under or beside the point of the axis whose value it stands for. The diagram is drawn again, and each of the seven nodes shows the new number in its own form. A slider has the keyboard too: Tab to it, and use the arrow keys, Home and End. You can also click the diagram itself to put the number where you clicked, rounded to the nearest tenth. A click is the plot node's own click handler, and it moves both sliders too.

The roots of unity

A root of unity is a complex number that, when raised to an integer power n, gives 1. These roots are evenly spaced round the complex unit circle, 360°/n apart.

These roots are used as the twiddle factors of an n-point Fourier transform. In the polar node, every modulus is exactly 1 and the angles go up in steps of 360°/n.

Move the slider to set the value of n.
