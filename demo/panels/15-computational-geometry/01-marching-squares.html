Marching Squares

A contour line joins the places where a surface has the same height. Given a grid of heights, the marching squares algorithm finds those lines one cell at a time. Each corner of a cell is either above a chosen level or below it, which gives 16 cases, and each case says which sides of the cell the line crosses and how the crossings are joined. Where it crosses a side, the point is found by interpolating between the two samples, and the two cells that share that side use the same point, so the lines meet exactly.

Two of the 16 cases are ambiguous: the two corners above the level are opposite each other, and the line could either join them or keep them apart. The surface between the corners settles it. The sign of Q = gTL·gBR − gTR·gBL, where g is a sample minus the level, says which way. If Q is exactly zero the two strands cross at the saddle of the surface.

Reading the picture

  • The three levels are told apart by their line as well as their colour: low is solid blue, middle is dashed amber, and high is dotted green.
  • The small grey dots are the samples. A red outline marks the cell being inspected.
  • A purple outline marks a cell where the case is ambiguous, once it has been visited.
  • Only the cells visited so far are drawn. A line that stops in the middle of the field is a provisional open end on the edge of the cells visited, and it is not reported as finished. A loop is called closed only when all of its cells have been visited.

Using the page

  • The controls are in the scene, above the plot, and they do not zoom with it.
  • Next and Previous visit one cell. Restart goes back to no cells. The range goes straight to any place, and its arrow keys move it one cell at a time. A button at an end is dimmed but can still be pressed, and then says so.
  • Field chooses the field. Ripple plane is the default, and the saddle lab has the five named cells. Show draws all three levels or one. Inspect chooses the level that the numbers are read at.
  • Threshold edits the level of the inspected slot. The three levels must stay in order, low then middle then high, and no sample may be exactly at a level. A value that is refused leaves everything as it was.
  • Row and Column choose a cell to inspect, and clicking the plot chooses the cell you click. A cell that has not been visited yet says so and shows nothing more.
  • Zoom and pan with the toolbar at the right of the plot, or hold Ctrl or Cmd and use the wheel. Nothing is worked out again when you zoom.
  • Rebuild scene makes the scene again from the state, and keeps the zoom, the pan, the step and the cell. Retry draws the page again if a write failed.
