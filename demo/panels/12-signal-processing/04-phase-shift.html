Phase Shift

The previous demo showed that a sine wave is the height of a point C as it rotates around a circle. A wave also has a phase. This is simply a measure of how far around the circle point C has travelled compared to some starting point.

The blue wave on the right is sin(x). It starts with C at zero degrees and never moves. The orange wave is sin(x + φ), where φ is the angle of C in the circle. At the left edge of the graph, the orange wave begins at the height of C, as if C had set out from that point on the circle instead.

Drag C round the circle, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys, and watch the orange wave slide along the blue one. A phase shift of φ degrees moves the wave to the left by φ degrees. A shift of 90° turns the sine wave into a cosine wave, and a shift of 180° turns it upside down.
