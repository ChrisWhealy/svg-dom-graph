Sparse Matrix–Vector Product: Scale and Resource Limits

The demo exists to discover the library’s resource limits, so its controls reach past them on purpose. Before any Scene call is made, the graph size you choose is checked against a budget. Any size over that budget is refused before any drawing takes place. Choose 20,000 entities or a label of 1,024 characters to see this.

What this demo shows: a computer often has to multiply a table of numbers (the matrix A) by a list of numbers [x] to make a new list [y]. Search algorithms, graphics rendering and machine learning all perform this type of task.

How to read it:

  1. Each row in y is made from one row of A
  2. Multiply every number in the row by the x in its column
  3. Then add the products.

The boxes at the right do exactly that for the selected row, and the bars draw y. Click another row to see the proecess operating on that row.

What a step does: A never changes. A step changes one number of x, and only the y values that use that x are recalculated, and they are outlined in green. Run keeps stepping, at the speed you choose. Steps go in order, x0 then x1 and so on, so you can tell what comes next. The Step order list can make it random.

Why it matters: most big tables are mostly zeros, so a computer keeps only the numbers that are not zero and skips the rest. Choose a larger size to see how much a page can draw before it slows down.

The demo opens in a window of its own, as large as your screen allows. The first scenario is small enough to read. A blue edge has a positive value and a red one a negative value, and the thicker the edge, the greater the value. Click a node to highlight its edges and to see the arithmetic it stands for, for example y3 = 5·x1 − 2·x7 = ….

Each step changes some entries of x and recomputes y from the change. The two data nodes are rewritten and the graph is left alone, and every step is checked against a full recomputation of the product whenever the matrix is small enough to afford it.

About the figures

  • They come from the browser you are using, on the machine you are using, and a timing is a measurement and not a promise.
  • The budget’s limits are from Chrome on one computer, recorded in spmv/docs/resource-risks.md.
  • The product is exact integer arithmetic, so it is compared with the oracle without any tolerance.
