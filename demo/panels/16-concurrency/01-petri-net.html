Petri Net: Bounded Producer–Consumer System

A Petri net consists of two types of elements: places (drawn as circles) and transitions (drawn as rectangles).

A place may hold zero or more tokens, and a transition is said to be enabled if all places connected to it as inputs contain at least one token.

Firing a transition transfers one token from each of those input places and adds one to each place it feeds, all at once.

Here four producers prepare items and put them into a buffer of eight slots, and three consumers take them out and process them. The buffer is two places, Free and Used, which always add up to eight. A Put needs a free slot and a Take needs a stored item, so nothing in the page stops a full buffer from taking another item. The net does, because the transition is not enabled.

Reading the picture

  • A place with a token: a circle with a solid dot. A place with none is an empty circle.
  • An enabled transition: a solid bar with a solid outline, and the word “enabled”.
  • A disabled transition: a pale bar with a dashed outline, and the word “disabled”.
  • A transition that has just become enabled: a double outline, and the word “new”.
  • The transition that just fired: a thick ring, and the word “fired”.
  • A worker that waits on the buffer: a dotted ring. A prepared item waits for a free slot, and an idle consumer waits for an item.
  • An arc: a line with an arrowhead showing its direction. Every arc has weight 1, so none is labelled.
  • The buffer: one filled cell for each used slot. The words say Used and Free as well.

Using the page

The selectors, the buttons and the range are in the scene, above the net.

  • Choose a scenario. Walkthrough fills the buffer, blocks a producer, releases it, drains the buffer and blocks the consumers. Contention has two producers compete for the last free slot. Interleaving keeps several workers busy, ready and processing at once. Choosing one shows its first snapshot on the same net.
  • Next and Previous move one event. Restart goes back to the first snapshot. A button that has nowhere to go is dimmed but can still be pressed, and then says so. The range goes straight to any snapshot, and a checkpoint goes to a named one.
  • Play steps through a script at the speed you choose and stops at the end. It never loops. While it plays, only a change in who waits on the buffer is announced to a screen reader.
  • Click a place or a transition, or move the keyboard focus to it, to see its inputs, outputs and tokens, and for a disabled transition, which input has no token. Selecting never fires anything.
  • Enter manual starts a branch from the snapshot you are on. In manual mode, select an enabled transition and press Fire selected, double-click it, or press Enter or Space while it has the focus. A disabled transition says what it is missing and changes nothing. Return to script goes back to the snapshot you left, and choosing a scenario discards the branch and says so.
  • Focused wiring dims the arcs that are not part of the selected place or transition. It changes how the arcs look and nothing else.
  • Tab to the net, which is a single stop, and use the arrow keys to move between the places and the transitions, and Home and End to the first and the last. The scene zooms and pans, and the controls do not.
