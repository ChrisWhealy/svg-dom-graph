Service Simulator: A Queue, Servers, Outages and Retries

Jobs arrive at a service. A job that finds a free server starts at once, and one that does not waits in a queue of a fixed size. A job arriving at a full queue is rejected.

A high priority job is taken from the queue before a low one. A server can go down, which interrupts the job that it holds, and a job whose attempt fails waits to retry until it has no attempt left. A job that is still not done at its deadline times out, and a job can be cancelled while it waits or runs.

Time is counted in whole ticks, and a snapshot is the state of the service after one more tick in which something happened. Snapshot 0 is the state before anything has. The run is the same every time it is chosen, since nothing in it is random.

Reading the picture

  • Server boxes say idle, down, or the job that they run and the tick at which it is due.
  • The queue boxes hold the jobs that wait, in the order that they will be taken, and each says high or low. A line says how many more wait than fit in the picture.
  • The Dispatch node stands for the dispatcher. It takes the job at the front of the queue, a high priority job before a low one, and gives it to a server that is idle. Each line to a server is a place that a job can go.
  • The counters are totals so far: arrived, admitted, rejected, completed, timed out, cancelled, failed, waiting to retry, stale pops and no-op pops.
  • Snapshot in eight numbers is a grid of eight cells that sums up the snapshot that you are on. Each row is named beside it. The first row is the snapshot and the simulated tick, the second is how many jobs wait and how many servers are busy, and the last two are the totals completed, timed out, cancelled and rejected. It is drawn as one of the library's data nodes, which is rewritten once for each snapshot, and the same numbers are in the inspector.
  • The selected job is outlined wherever it is, and its line at the bottom says where.

Using the page

  • Next and Previous move one snapshot. Restart goes back to snapshot 0. A button that has nowhere to go is dimmed but can still be pressed, and then says so.
  • Play steps at the speed that you choose and stops at the end. It never loops, and it never starts again on its own after the panel has been hidden.
  • The Snapshot range goes straight to any snapshot. Each arrow key is a request of its own, and the last one wins.
  • Choosing another scenario starts it at snapshot 0. If it cannot be shown, the one that you had stays as it was, and the page says why. The advanced scenario is a long run of 5,464 snapshots, and it is not a benchmark.
  • Hiding the panel, or the page, stops Play, and it is never started again for you. The page says that it paused, and Play resumes from where it stopped.
  • Repair display draws the snapshot that you are on again, in full. It never moves, selects or plays.
  • Inspect a job outlines a job that is in the picture and says what state it is in. Choose None to clear it.
