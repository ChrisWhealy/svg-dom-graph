Red–Black Tree

A binary search tree gets slow when its keys arrive in order, because it grows into a line. A red–black tree stays short by colouring every node red or black and keeping a few rules: the root is black, a red node never has a red child, and every path from the root down to an empty place passes the same number of black nodes.

When an insertion or a deletion breaks a rule, the tree repairs itself by recolouring and by rotating. A rotation changes which node is the parent of which, and moves one subtree across, without losing the order of the keys. This run inserts 10, 5, 1, 15, 20 and 12, and then deletes 15, which has two children, so its place is taken by the next key in order, the original node 20, which is moved and not copied.

Reading the picture

  • Each circle says its key and its colour: B for black and R for red, so the colour is also written.
  • A line runs from a parent to its child, and its arrowhead points at the child.
  • A solid blue ring marks the key you selected. A dashed orange ring marks the key that has the keyboard focus.
  • A step in the middle of a repair says Repair in progress, because the tree may break a rule until the repair ends. A step that ends an insertion or a deletion says it is a valid red–black tree.

Using the page

  • The buttons and the range are in the scene, above the tree.
  • Next and Previous move one event. Restart goes back to the empty tree. A button that has nowhere to go is dimmed but can still be pressed, and then says so.
  • The three jump buttons go to the state just after a rotation or choice: the right rotation at 10 and the left rotation at 10 while building, and the choice of 20 as the successor of 15.
  • Play steps through the run twice a second and stops at the end. Any other button, or the range, stops it. While it plays, only finished operations, rotations, the transplant and the repair cases are announced to a screen reader. Pausing says where the run is.
  • Selecting a key remembers your choice. If you select 15 and play past its deletion, 20 is shown as selected and the note says why. Going back shows 15 again.
