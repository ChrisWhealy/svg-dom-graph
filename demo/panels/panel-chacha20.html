ChaCha20

ChaCha20 is a stream cipher. It turns a 256-bit key, a 96-bit nonce and a block counter into a stream of pseudo-random bytes, and XORs that keystream into the message. Decrypting is the same operation.

This implementation follows the IETF version of ChaCha20 specified in RFC 8439, rather than Bernstein's original variant.

The keystream comes from a state of sixteen 32-bit words, drawn at the top as a 4 by 4 matrix. The first four words are fixed constants, the next eight are the key, then comes the counter, and the last three are the nonce. Twenty rounds mix the state, and each round applies the quarter round to four of its words.

The quarter round takes four words, a, b, c and d, and mixes them with three operations: addition, XOR and rotation. It has 12 steps:

        a += b; d ^= a; d <<<= 16;
            c += d; b ^= c; b <<<= 12;
            a += b; d ^= a; d <<<= 8;
            c += d; b ^= c; b <<<= 7;

Each word runs down a column of its own. Every step is an operator node in the column of the word it updates: ADD for +=, XOR for ^= and ROTL for <<<=. An ADD or XOR takes the latest value of a second word, so its connector crosses from that word's column.

Odd rounds apply the quarter round to the four columns of the state, and even rounds apply it to the four diagonals. The demo walks through all 20 rounds, one quarter round at a time. At the top is the state before the quarter round, with the four words it mixes marked, and its rows are labelled a to d. Those four words feed the columns. The four words that come out make up the state below. The state of RFC 8439, section 2.3.2, is the first. After the 80th quarter round, one more step shows the keystream block, which is the state after 20 rounds added word by word to the initial state.

Every value shown is a real result, worked out in plain Rust. The demo's own tests check the quarter round, the whole block function and a complete encryption against the RFC's published values.

"Next" and "Prev" move one step, and the "Round" buttons move one whole round. The current step and the values it reads are highlighted. Anything the walk has not reached yet shows zero and is drawn faint, and "Restart" returns every node to that state.
