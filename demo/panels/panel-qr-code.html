QR Code: the Matrix

A QR code is a square grid of dark and light small squares known as "modules". A Version 1 symbol is 21 by 21, which is 441 modules.

This demo shows the first part of a QR code demo: the grid, and nothing else. It shows how the matrix will be drawn, and it encodes no text. The bits it places are a fixed sample.

Each module has a colour and functions in a particular role, and either can be known first. The finder, separator, timing and dark modules are all known from the start. The format modules are reserved with them, so their role is known, but their colour will not be allocated until the last step. A data module has neither until a codeword is placed in it. A module without a colour is drawn pale blue, so it cannot be taken for a light one.

The Prev, Next and Restart buttons step through five states.

Choose a role from the list to highlight every module that performs this role, and to read what the role is for. The blue outline marks the modules the step changed, and the orange outline marks the role.

The diagram is drawn with the same few SVG elements at every step, however many modules have a colour.
